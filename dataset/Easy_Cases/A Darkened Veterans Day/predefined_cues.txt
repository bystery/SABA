The Crime: Pure gold ceremonial Veterans' pins were stolen from a locked room at City Hall during the town's tenth annual Veterans Day celebration.

The Delivery: Ryan Smith, the delivery boy, brought the pins in a plain cardboard box.

Verification: Both Mr. Landry and Frank Thompson (the custodian) verified the contents and the engravings immediately after delivery, confirming the pins were present before being locked away.

Security: The pins were placed in a room to which only the custodian, Frank Thompson, held the key.

Access: Colonel Abraham borrowed the key from Frank Thompson to retrieve the box for the ceremony, claiming he found the box empty upon entering.

Physical Evidence: The police officer discovered a "plug of tobacco" (unused chewing tobacco) on the floor near the empty cardboard box.

Frank Thompson’s Habit: Frank was actively chewing tobacco during the investigation. However, he stated he does not normally keep tobacco on him; he was only chewing a small amount that Colonel Abraham had given him earlier.

Colonel Abraham’s Supply: Since the custodian had no supply of his own and only had what was currently in his mouth, the unused plug found on the floor must have belonged to the provider.

The Slip-up: The unused plug of tobacco likely fell out of the Colonel's pocket while he was hastily emptying the box of gold pins.

Suspect 1 (Ryan Smith): Ruled out because the pins were verified by Landry and Thompson after he delivered them.

Suspect 2 (Mr. Landry): Ruled out as he had no unique access to the locked room and noted his father had little interest in the pins.

Suspect 3 (Frank Thompson): Although he had the key, the discovery of the unused tobacco plug—which he did not possess—pointed away from him.

The Reveal: The presence of the unused tobacco at the crime scene, coupled with the fact that the Colonel was the source of the tobacco and the last person to have the key, proved he was the thief.

Outcome: The stolen pins were recovered from the Colonel’s truck, and he was arrested.
