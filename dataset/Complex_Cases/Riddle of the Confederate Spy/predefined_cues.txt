Environmental Context: A Union sergeant and four soldiers are guarding a secluded railroad bridge in Illinois during the Civil War in 1863 (referenced by the Maryland campaign).

The Crime: A bundle of dynamite was found on the bridge, intended for sabotage to stop Union supplies from reaching the front lines.

Access and Timing: The bridge is isolated with no way to approach it unseen; therefore, the saboteur must be one of the four soldiers who arrived during the week.

The Dynamite Trap: Sergeant Stoker brandished the dynamite and claimed it was found "on" the bridge, but he withheld the specific placement to test the suspects.

Suspect 1 (McMurty): Ruled out because he slept beside the campfire. It is illogical for someone hiding a pack of explosives to sleep next to an open flame; additionally, his position was too "in plain sight" for a saboteur.

Suspect 2 (Garrett): Claimed to be from Ohio and slept under a tree; his arrival was delayed by a "lazy horse," which did not present a logical contradiction.

Suspect 3 (Winslow): Claimed to be from Indiana and slept in front of a bush; he correctly identified Abraham Lincoln as President.

Geographic Inconsistency (Parker): Parker claimed to have traveled from West Virginia to Illinois (approx. 600 miles) in less than a week. The sergeant calculated that this would require 85 miles of travel per day, whereas even the best horses max out at 50 miles.

Historical/Geographic Error (Parker): Parker claimed to "log incoming ships at a receiving house" in West Virginia. The sergeant pointed out that West Virginia is landlocked and does not touch the ocean.

The Slip of the Tongue (Parker): While the sergeant stated the dynamite was "on" the bridge, Parker joked about a rebel sticking dynamite "under our bridge." This revealed his specific knowledge of the exact placement of the explosives.

Concealment Choice: Parker chose to sleep "behind a rock," a position that offered the most cover for someone trying to hide a pack of dynamite or slip away unnoticed at night.

The Motive: Parker likely worked at a Southern port blocked by General Winfield Scott’s "Anaconda Plan," giving him a reason to support the Confederacy.

The Arrest: Based on the impossible travel time, the geographic lie regarding West Virginia, and the slip of the tongue regarding the dynamite's location, Parker was identified as the spy.
