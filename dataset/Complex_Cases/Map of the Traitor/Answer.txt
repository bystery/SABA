“It must be Lucius,” said one soldier, “since he hates the American rebels.”
“But if I were trying to escape, I wouldn't reveal that I supported the British,” Nate said. “Besides, he was clearly just 17 years old. He's too young to be a major in the British army!”
“I thought Edward looked suspicious,” another soldier suggested, “since he kept trying to remind us how long he'd been farming.”
“He's a hard-working farmer,” Nate replied, “and besides, he's traveling in his bare feet. The messenger who warned us said that our British major tucked the map into his boot.”
“Perhaps the third traveler, Benjamin, isn't really traveling to Charleston,” another soldier suggested, “and instead of a shady tree, he's galloping straight towards his British commander!”
“But he was riding a horse,” Nate pointed out. “Our British major has been traveling by foot, according to the messenger. And, in fact, he told us that the major has been traveling since midnight, with no time for sleeping. I bet he's tired now, and he's got bloodshot eyes surrounded by dark circles.”
He pointed the men towards Jonathan, who ran as he saw the soldiers approaching. But the men on horseback quickly surrounded him, searched his boots, and discovered the map to the fort. They also discovered a letter he'd received from an American general named Benedict Arnold. Arnold bragged that he's had himself placed in command of the fort — but only so he could surrender it to the British!
It would've been a horrible setback for the American cause. But now that they'd recovered the map and discovered Arnold's secret plan, the fort at West Point would remain safe from attack; and so would the American army.
But as the sun crept higher into the Virginia sky, Nate was happy for another reason: He finally had some good news to bring to General Washington.
