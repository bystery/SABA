“Yeah, it was my stupid little brother, Mark!” Matthew blurted. “We caught him riding around on my bike before we left – remember?”
“I remember,” String replied. “But would the kid be riding a bike with a loosened front wheel – if he knew about it?”
“Yeah, I guess even he’s not that dumb,” Matthew reluctantly agreed. He glared at String. “But what about this practical joke business?”
“I already explained that away. I would have made sure that I kept up to see it happen if I had known about it.” String explained again. “Plus, I make sure my jokes could never actually hurt anyone, like this stunt could’ve.”
Matthew fingered Cory next. “What about this guy? He picked the trails – took us down all those bumpy paths that were sure to jolt my loose wheel right off. Plus, he’s resented my new bike from the start!”
“We’ve all resented your attitude about the new bike,” String stated. “Not letting any of us ride it. But Cory wasn’t at your house to do the dirty deed. He was already here waiting for us in the park.”
String paused dramatically. “But one other person was at your house, had the opportunity to commit the crime, along with the motivation. Not to mention the weapon – the tools from your own garage. Right … David?”
The redhead’s freckled face burned a guilty shade of crimson. “Well … Matthew was being such a knob about his new bike and all. I-I just wanted to teach him a lesson.”
Matthew sighed and walked over to his upended bicycle. “Lesson taught,” he conceded. “Now, c’mon and help me fix my bike, and I’ll let you all ride it.”
