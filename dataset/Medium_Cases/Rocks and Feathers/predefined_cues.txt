Setting: A Pennsylvania safe house on the Underground Railroad in 1848, managed by Reverend Elijah Gray, a free black man.

The Victim: Ro, a refugee accused by others of stealing money from fellow slaves. To prevent conflict, Rev. Gray hid him in a secret room within the chicken coop.

The Crime: Ro was found dead outside the coop from a blow to the head with a stone. Feathers littered the ground.

Suspect 1 (Bertha): Found with a blood-stained dress. She claimed she killed Ro for his money, but the Reverend deduced this was a lie because she knew the money was sewn into his jacket yet didn't take it.

Suspects 2 & 3 (Barley and Joseph): They had a clear grudge against Ro. However, they were ruled out because Rev. Gray had kept Ro’s sleeping location a secret; they had no way of knowing he was in the chicken coop.

Suspect 4 (Tom): Bertha’s son, described as "simple" with a "fearful temper" due to a childhood head injury.

Physical Evidence 1 (The Feathers): Tom appeared with feathers all over his shoes, and feathers were found at the crime scene (the chicken coop).

Physical Evidence 2 (The Window): Tom admitted to escaping the cellar through the unlatched "swinging window" mentioned earlier by the Reverend.

Motive/Logic: Tom entered the coop because he wanted to "fetch a chicken" for his mother. Ro, sleeping there, tried to stop him. Tom, possessing a "fearful temper" and perceiving Ro as a "bad man" who would "tell on him," struck Ro with a stone.

The Reveal: Tom’s own nervous confession—"I hit the bad man! Don't tell nobody!"—confirmed he was the killer. Bertha had only arrived afterward, staining her dress while trying to help Ro or cover for her son.

Outcome: Rev. Gray showed mercy, recognizing the tragedy of the situation. He provided Bertha and Tom with supplies and a map to continue their journey to freedom.
