“Ro tried to stop you and you hit him with the stone, didn’t you?” Rev. Gray said.
Bertha began to protest, but he interrupted her: “No, you didn’t do it, Bertha. You knew where the money was, yet you didn’t take it. And Joseph and Barley couldn’t have killed Ro because they didn’t know where he was sleeping.”
Rev. Gray looked at Tom.
“Bad man shouted,” Tom blurted nervously. “Gonna tell on me! So I hit the bad man! Don't tell nobody!"
Bertha rushed to her son and held his head to her shoulder.
“I...I didn't get there in time,” Bertha said in between sobs. “Ro was dead 'fore I got to him.”

Epilogue:
That evening, the passengers on the Underground Railroad gathered in a distant corner of the lot to pay their last respects to Ro. Rev. Gray saw the distant figures of Bertha and Tom, hunched over and fearful, running along the roadside until they reached the woods. He had given them money, a few days worth of bread and cheese, and a map to guide their way to freedom.
